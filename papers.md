# Reading shelf

Papers I’ve read, in the order I added them. Each has a one-line takeaway; some have longer notes.

- [Attention Is All You Need](https://arxiv.org/abs/1706.03762), Vaswani et al., NeurIPS 2017: The transformer architecture dispenses with recurrence and convolutions entirely, relying solely on attention mechanisms — a deceptively simple idea that reshaped the entire field.
  - What it does: Proposes the Transformer — a sequence-to-sequence architecture built entirely on self-attention, with no RNNs or CNNs. Encoder and decoder stacks of multi-head attention + feedforward layers, connected by residual paths and layer norms.
  - Why it matters: It became the backbone of BERT, GPT, and virtually every modern LLM. By removing recurrence, training became fully parallelizable — making it practical to scale to massive datasets and model sizes.
  - How it works: Multi-head self-attention lets each token attend to all others simultaneously. Positional encodings inject sequence order. The encoder maps inputs to a continuous representation; the decoder generates outputs autoregressively attending to both.
  - Key result: SOTA BLEU on WMT'14 English-German and English-French translation, trained in a fraction of the time of prior RNN/CNN models. The quality-to-compute tradeoff was just different enough to shift the whole paradigm.
- [A Watermark for Large Language Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.10226), Kirchenbauer et al., ICML 2023: Embeds a statistical signal into LLM outputs by partitioning the vocabulary into "green" and "red" lists at each token step — elegant in its simplicity, and surprisingly robust to paraphrasing attacks.
  - What it does: Introduces a practical watermarking scheme for LLM-generated text. At generation time, the model is biased to favor tokens from a "green list" derived by hashing the previous token. A z-test on the green-token fraction detects the watermark.
  - Why it matters: Provides the first practical, model-agnostic method to detect AI-generated text without model access. Directly relevant to AI misuse, academic integrity, and provenance tracking. Sparked a whole subfield.
  - How it works: Before sampling each token, they hash the previous token ID to seed a PRNG, then partition the vocab into a green list (50%) and red list. At generation, logits for green tokens get a soft boost δ. Detection is a one-sided z-test on green-token proportion.
  - Key result: >95% detection power on sequences of 200+ tokens with a false positive rate near 0.01%, and less than 1% perplexity degradation. The watermark survives moderate paraphrasing, which was the surprising part.
- [Distilling Step-by-Step!](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.02301), Hsieh et al., ACL Findings 2023: Uses chain-of-thought rationales from large models as a supervision signal for smaller ones — a clever way to transfer reasoning capabilities, not just final answers.
  - What it does: A knowledge distillation framework where a large teacher LLM generates chain-of-thought rationales that then serve as auxiliary supervision for a small student model. The student learns to predict both the label and the reasoning chain.
  - Why it matters: Shows that a 770M-parameter model trained this way outperforms a 540B teacher (PaLM) on several benchmarks — using orders of magnitude less data than standard fine-tuning. Challenges the assumption that task performance requires scale.
  - How it works: Multi-task learning: the student has one head predicting the label, another predicting the rationale. Both losses are optimized together. The teacher (PaLM 540B) provides rationales via few-shot prompting at dataset construction time, not at inference.
  - Key result: Outperforms standard fine-tuning and distillation baselines across NLI, commonsense QA, and arithmetic tasks, with significantly less labeled data. The rationale supervision is the differentiating factor — labels alone don't close the gap.
- [Invariant Risk Minimization](https://arxiv.org/abs/1907.02893), Arjovsky et al., arXiv 2019: Proposes learning data representations where the optimal classifier is invariant across training environments — a principled approach to OOD generalization that fundamentally challenged ERM assumptions.
  - What it does: Introduces IRM, a learning paradigm that finds a feature representation Φ such that the optimal classifier on top of Φ is the same across all training environments. This forces the model to rely on causal, not spurious, features.
  - Why it matters: ERM (standard training) exploits any correlation in the training data, including spurious ones that don't hold at test time. IRM is a principled attempt to do causal learning from observational data — directly relevant to robustness and fairness.
  - How it works: Bi-level optimization: minimize ERM loss while also penalizing the gradient of a fixed dummy classifier (a scalar 1.0 on Φ). If the gradient is nonzero, it means the classifier isn't optimal everywhere — a sign of non-invariance. IRMv1 relaxes this as a penalty term.
  - Key result: Recovers causal features on ColoredMNIST (a spurious-correlation benchmark) where ERM completely fails. However, real-world results (e.g., WILDS benchmarks) are mixed, and later work showed IRM underperforms ERM in many practical settings. Important caveat.
- Sequential Change-Point Detection via Online Convex Optimization, Hazan et al., 2023: Frames change-point detection as an online learning problem, achieving near-optimal detection delay with clean regret-style guarantees — a refreshing new lens on a classical statistics problem.
  - What it does: Reformulates sequential change-point detection as an instance of online convex optimization (OCO). The CUSUM statistic emerges naturally from an online mirror descent update, allowing regret analysis to bound detection delay.
  - Why it matters: Classical CUSUM theory requires parametric assumptions (known pre/post distributions). OCO framing enables data-adaptive, nonparametric detection with rigorous delay guarantees — a cleaner theoretical foundation for modern applications.
  - How it works: Define a loss function tied to the log-likelihood ratio. Run online mirror descent on this sequence of losses. Show that the cumulative loss until a stopping rule triggers has O(log T) regret relative to the optimal fixed-threshold strategy.
  - Key result: Near-optimal expected detection delay under Gaussian mean shift, matching the Lorden lower bound up to log factors. The connection to OCO also gives a clean modular way to plug in different divergences or loss structures.
- [Language Models are Few-Shot Learners](https://arxiv.org/abs/2005.14165), Brown et al., NeurIPS 2020: GPT-3 demonstrates that scale alone unlocks emergent few-shot behavior — raising more questions about what "learning" really means than it actually answers.
  - What it does: Trains GPT-3 (175B parameters) on a massive diverse text corpus and evaluates it in zero-, one-, and few-shot settings — where "few-shot" means in-context examples in the prompt, no gradient updates.
  - Why it matters: Demonstrated that large-scale pretraining alone produces a model capable of adapting to arbitrary tasks via prompting. Spawned the prompt engineering era and raised deep questions about generalization, memorization, and what in-context learning actually is.
  - How it works: Standard autoregressive LM training on ~300B tokens from Common Crawl, WebText, Books, and Wikipedia. Evaluation is purely inference-time: condition on k examples (k=0,1,few) and score the model's completion. No fine-tuning whatsoever.
  - Key result: Few-shot GPT-3 matches or beats fine-tuned SOTA on SuperGLUE tasks. Performance scales smoothly and predictably with model size, context length, and data — the scaling laws paper companion made this quantitative.
- [Small Language Models (SLMs) Can Still Pack a Punch: A Survey](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.05465), Gamage et al., arXiv 2025: A comprehensive survey showing that sub-10B models can match or exceed much larger counterparts when trained intelligently — covering architecture choices, data curation, and post-training techniques.
- [Training Compute-Optimal Large Language Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.15556), Hoffmann et al., arXiv 2022: Shows most LLMs are severely undertrained — optimal compute allocation requires scaling data proportionally with model size, yielding the Chinchilla recipe: 70B params, 1.4T tokens.
- [On the Slow Death of Scaling](https://ssrn.com/abstract=5877662), Hooker, SSRN 2025: A critical examination of diminishing returns from scaling compute alone, arguing that continued progress requires architectural innovation, data quality improvements, and algorithmic efficiency gains.
- [Baguettotron: A 321M Parameter Generative Model](https://huggingface.co/PleIAs/Baguettotron), PleIAs, Hugging Face 2024: A 321M-parameter open French generative language model trained from scratch, demonstrating that high-quality small models can be built for non-English languages with modest compute.
- [Textbooks Are All You Need](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.11644), Gunasekar et al., arXiv 2023: Trains phi-1, a 1.3B coding LLM, purely on high-quality synthetic "textbook-like" data — demonstrating that data quality can substitute for scale with far fewer tokens than conventional pretraining.
- [Scaling Laws for Optimal Data Mixtures](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.09404), Shukor et al., arXiv 2025: Derives scaling laws for data mixing ratios across domains, showing that the optimal mixture itself shifts with compute budget and cannot be fixed once for all training runs.
- [Topic Over Source: The Key to Effective Data Mixing for Language Models Pre-training](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.16802), Zhu et al., arXiv 2025: Finds that grouping pretraining data by topic rather than by source domain leads to substantially more effective mixing strategies, challenging conventional domain-based data curation pipelines.
- [SmolLM2 Technical Report](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.02737), Allal et al., arXiv 2025: HuggingFace's family of compact language models (135M–1.7B) trained on carefully curated datasets — a practical and transparent reference point for efficient small-model training.
- [The Smol Training Playbook](https://huggingface.co/spaces/HuggingFaceTB/smol-training-playbook), Ben Allal et al., Hugging Face 2024: A practitioner's guide from HuggingFace covering the full recipe for training small language models: data curation, architecture choices, training dynamics, and evaluation strategies.
- [Hymba: A Hybrid-head Architecture for Small Language Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.13676), NVIDIA, arXiv 2024: Combines Mamba state-space heads with attention heads in the same layer, achieving strong performance with a lower memory footprint than pure-attention models at small scales.
- [Eagle and Finch: RWKV with Matrix-Valued States and Dynamic Recurrence](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.05892), Peng et al., arXiv 2024: Extends RWKV with matrix-valued hidden states and dynamic recurrence gating, closing much of the quality gap with transformers while preserving linear-time inference.
- [MobiLlama: Towards Accurate and Lightweight Fully Transparent GPT](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.16840), Thawakar et al., arXiv 2024: A fully transparent 0.5B-parameter LLM designed for on-device deployment, using parameter sharing across all transformer layers to dramatically reduce model size without large quality losses.
- [OpenELM: An Efficient Language Model Family](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.14619), Mehta et al., arXiv 2024: Apple's open-source model family using layer-wise scaling of attention heads and FFN dimensions, achieving competitive performance with fewer total parameters than uniformly-sized counterparts.
- [Conditional Memory via Scalable Lookup: A New Axis of Sparsity for Large Language Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.07372), Cheng et al., arXiv 2026: Replaces dense attention's KV cache with a sparse lookup over a large external memory bank, introducing a new axis of sparsity that enables long-context modeling at dramatically lower memory cost.
- [FlexOLMo: Open Language Models for Flexible Data Use](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.07024), Shi et al., arXiv 2025: An OLMo variant demonstrating that flexible data-licensing policies — mixing open and restricted sources with explicit provenance — can be incorporated without degrading model quality.
- [Front-Loading Reasoning: The Synergy between Pretraining and Post-Training Data](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.03264), NVIDIA and Hugging Face, arXiv 2025: Shows that including reasoning-rich data early in pretraining — rather than deferring it to post-training — creates stronger base models that respond more effectively to RLHF and instruction tuning.
- [Midtraining Bridges Pretraining and Posttraining Distributions](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.14865), Liu et al., arXiv 2025: Introduces a midtraining phase that sits between pretraining and finetuning, reducing the distributional gap between the two and improving both instruction following and domain adaptation.
- [Balancing the Budget: Understanding Trade-offs Between Supervised and Preference-Based Finetuning](https://aclanthology.org/2025.acl-long.1248.pdf), Raghavendra et al., ACL 2025: Analyzes the compute tradeoff between SFT and preference-based finetuning (DPO/RLHF) under a fixed budget, finding that SFT-heavy allocations generally outperform preference-heavy ones.
- [The Llama 3 Herd of Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.21783), Dubey et al., arXiv 2024: Meta's comprehensive report on Llama 3 — covering pretraining data pipeline, architecture decisions, the full post-training stack, and safety work across 8B, 70B, and 405B scales.
- [DeepSeek-V3 Technical Report](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.19437), DeepSeek-AI, arXiv 2024: A 671B mixture-of-experts model achieving GPT-4-level performance at significantly lower training cost, with innovations in MoE load balancing, multi-token prediction, and FP8 training.
- [Data Curation via Joint Example Selection Further Accelerates Multimodal Learning (JEST)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.17711), Evans et al., arXiv 2024: Scores training examples by their joint utility for an entire mini-batch rather than individually, enabling more efficient pretraining convergence with substantially less data.
- [DeepSeek-V3.2-Exp: Boosting Long-Context Efficiency with DeepSeek Sparse Attention](https://github.com/deepseek-ai/DeepSeek-V3.2-Exp/blob/main/DeepSeek_V3_2.pdf), DeepSeek-AI, 2025: Extends DeepSeek-V3 with sparse attention patterns that enable efficient long-context processing, reducing KV cache memory and compute without proportional quality losses.
- [OLMoE: Open Mixture-of-Experts Language Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.02060), Muennighoff et al., arXiv 2024: A fully open-sourced sparse MoE language model — releasing weights, training data, and code — establishing a transparency benchmark for mixture-of-experts research.
- [Explaining Context Length Scaling and Bounds for Language Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.01481), Chen et al., arXiv 2025: Derives theoretical bounds on how context length affects LLM performance, providing a principled explanation for the empirical scaling behavior observed in long-context model evaluations.
- [Sample-Efficient Language Modeling with Linear Attention and Lightweight Enhancements](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.05560), Haller et al., arXiv 2025: Combines linear attention with a set of lightweight architectural enhancements to match standard transformer performance while requiring significantly fewer training tokens.
- [LIFT: Improving Long Context Understanding via Long Input Fine-Tuning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.14644), Wang et al., arXiv 2025: A fine-tuning approach for extending LLM context windows that trains the model to attend to information spread across long inputs, improving retrieval accuracy and coherence on long-document tasks.
- [Phi-4 Technical Report](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.08905), Microsoft Research, arXiv 2024: Microsoft's 14B model trained with a heavy emphasis on synthetic data generation and careful data curation, demonstrating again that data quality consistently outpunches raw scale.
- [MiniCPM: Unveiling the Potential of Small Language Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.06395), Hu et al., arXiv 2024: A family of sub-3B models from Tsinghua/ModelBest using scaling-law-guided training decisions — showing the 1-2B regime can be highly competitive with careful hyperparameter and data choices.
- [ORPO: Monolithic Preference Optimization without Reference Model](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.07691), Hong et al., arXiv 2024: Eliminates the reference model from preference optimization by incorporating an odds-ratio penalty directly into the SFT loss — a cleaner, cheaper alternative to DPO and PPO.
- [Language Modeling Is Compression](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.10668), Delétang et al., arXiv 2023: Formally proves that language modeling and data compression are equivalent — a model's perplexity directly determines its compression ratio, unifying the two fields under information theory.
